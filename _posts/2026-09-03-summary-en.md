## [Google Avoids Government-Mandated Breakup of Ad Tech Business](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/02/technology/google-ad-tech-remedies.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Google has successfully fended off a government-mandated breakup of its ad technology business, a significant outcome in a long-running antitrust case. This decision means the company will not be forced to divest parts of its ad tech operations, maintaining its current structure. This ruling has major implications for antitrust enforcement and the future of the tech industry, potentially signaling a higher bar for regulators seeking to break up dominant companies. It affects how competition is regulated in the digital advertising market and could influence future antitrust challenges against other tech giants. Google's ad tech business generated $30 billion last year, representing about 8 percent of its parent company Alphabet's revenue, despite its ad tech revenue declining for 16 consecutive quarters. Analysts estimate this segment accounts for less than 1 percent of the company’s profit, leading some to question its overall significance.

hackernews · donohoe · Sep 2, 14:46 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49537131)

**Background**: Antitrust laws are designed to promote fair competition in markets, prevent monopolies, and protect consumers from anti-competitive practices. Governments use these laws to investigate companies that might be abusing their market power, with potential remedies ranging from fines to forced divestitures or breakups of businesses. The goal is to ensure that no single entity gains excessive control over an industry, which could stifle innovation or lead to higher prices.

**Discussion**: The community expressed frustration over the difficulty of breaking up large companies, suggesting that legislation should make it as easy to unmerge companies as it is to merge them, or progressively tax monopolies. Some questioned the reported low profitability of Google's ad tech business, while others lamented the consistent ability of tech giants to avoid significant regulatory consequences.
